Evidence

We read your customer journey before we ever contact you.

Every audit is built from what a customer actually sees: the website, the Google listing, the reviews, the enquiry flow. Three findings from Hyderabad businesses, exactly as they were found.

Finding 01
Clinic · KPHB

“Make an appointment” led to a page with no form.

The button worked and the page loaded, but there was nothing to fill in — so anyone trying to book online left.

Why it mattersA treatment course is worth tens of thousands. Interest was being paid for and then lost.

Finding 02
Spa · Gachibowli

A customer booked online and the centre never received it.

They arrived expecting a confirmed appointment, were told it had not come through, and later left a public one-star review naming the booking system.

Why it mattersOne lost appointment is a few thousand rupees. A public review about a broken system costs far more.

Finding 03
Banquet venue · Karwan

Thousands of reviews and no website of its own.

Every enquiry arrived through third-party portals rather than a channel the venue controlled — on bookings priced in lakhs.

Why it mattersPortal enquiries carry commission and build no relationship. Direct enquiries do both.

The pattern

Thirty-five businesses. Five unrelated niches. One failure.

Across clinics, salons, fitness studios, venues and coaching centres — businesses with nothing else in common — the same defect kept appearing: nothing acknowledges an enquiry immediately.

Dead booking buttons

A prominent “Book now” or “Make an appointment” call to action leading to a page with no form behind it. The most common single defect we found.

Leads into personal inboxes

Enquiries routed to an individual's Gmail or WhatsApp rather than a shared, monitored channel — so they are answered when that one person looks.

Business-hours-only response

Enquiries arriving in the evening are answered the next morning. In a market where customers contact several providers at once, that is usually too late.

Quotes with no follow-up

A price is sent and never revisited. This is the quietest loss, because nothing appears broken — the enquiry simply stops.

Reviews never requested

Satisfied customers are not asked, so a good business's rating reflects who happened to post rather than the work done.

No single view of the pipeline

Enquiries split across phone, WhatsApp, walk-ins and portals, with no way to tell who was contacted, quoted or booked.

What we install

The enquiry that is answered first usually wins.

We do not sell software and we do not sell advertising. We install the five things that stop a business losing interest it has already paid for.

01 — Acknowledge

Every enquiry is answered within minutes.

Website forms, WhatsApp messages and missed calls all receive an immediate reply, day or night — including enquiries that arrive while the business is closed.

02 — Qualify

Two or three questions, asked automatically.

What the customer needs and when. Staff then spend their time on people ready to buy rather than sorting an inbox.

03 — Book

Appointments confirmed, then reminded.

Confirmation and reminder messages go out without anyone having to remember — which is what removes most no-shows.

04 — Follow up

Quiet quotes get chased.

If an enquiry goes silent after a quote, it is followed up on a schedule instead of quietly buying elsewhere.

05 — Ask

Reviews are requested at the right moment.

After a good visit, not in a bulk email. Your rating then reflects your work rather than who happened to remember to post.

Method

How each finding is labelled.

Because a claim you cannot check is not evidence.

Observed

Seen directly on a public page, listing or review: a form that does not submit, a policy of answering only in business hours, a public complaint about a booking.

Inferred

A reasonable conclusion drawn from what was observed — for example, that a missing reminder contributes to no-shows. Stated as inference, never as fact.

Unknown

What we cannot see from outside — internal processes, private message volume, actual conversion rate. We say so rather than estimating.
