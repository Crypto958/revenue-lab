## 1. The statutory framework (what the primary texts say)

### 1.1 Type of vehicle and type of use
- **FINDING** — The Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 ("MV Act"), s.2(7), defines **contract carriage** as a motor vehicle that "carries a passenger or passengers for hire or reward and is engaged under a contract … for the use of such vehicle as a whole for the carriage of passengers mentioned therein … on a fixed or an agreed rate or sum— (a) on a time basis … or (b) from one point to another, and in either case, **without stopping to pick up or set down passengers not included in the contract anywhere during the journey**". A 17-seat Urbania hired as a whole for a group fits this description. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.2(40) defines **stage carriage** as a vehicle "constructed or adapted to carry more than six passengers excluding the driver for hire or reward **at separate fares paid by or for individual passengers**, either for the whole journey or for stages of the journey". [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.2(35) defines **public service vehicle** (PSV) as any vehicle used for carriage of passengers for hire or reward, including maxicab, motorcab, contract carriage and stage carriage. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.2(47) defines **transport vehicle** as a public service vehicle, a goods carriage, an educational institution bus or a private service vehicle. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.2(22) defines **maxicab** as carrying "more than six passengers, but not more than twelve passengers, excluding the driver". A 17-seat vehicle is therefore **not** a maxicab. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.2(43) defines **tourist vehicle** as "a contract carriage constructed or adapted and equipped and maintained in accordance with such specifications as may be prescribed". [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.2(1A) defines **aggregator** as "a digital intermediary or market place for a passenger to connect with a driver for the purpose of transportation". [S1]

### 1.2 The permit requirement
- **FINDING** — MV Act s.66(1): "No owner of a motor vehicle shall use or permit the use of the vehicle **as a transport vehicle** in any public place … save in accordance with the conditions of a permit granted or countersigned by a Regional or State Transport Authority or any prescribed authority". s.66 has limited exemptions in s.66(3) (government/local-authority/private-use categories); none of them covers carrying paying passengers for hire. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.73 (application) and s.74 (grant) govern a **contract carriage permit**; granted by the Regional Transport Authority (RTA), area-specific, with conditions the RTA may attach (route/area, maximum passengers/luggage, rates for non-motorcab vehicles, etc.). [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.81: a permit (other than a temporary permit under s.87 or a special permit under s.88(8)) is effective "for a period of five years" from issue/renewal. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.84 sets out **general conditions attaching to every permit**, including that the vehicle carries a valid certificate of fitness under s.56 and that the operator's name and address is painted/affixed on the vehicle. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.88(1): a permit is **not valid outside the region/State in which it was granted** unless countersigned by the RTA of the other region or the State Transport Authority (STA) of the other State. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.88(8) allows a **special permit** for a PSV for a contract journey, valid in other regions/States without countersignature. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.88(9): any STA may, for promoting tourism, "grant permits in respect of tourist vehicles valid for the whole of India, or in such contiguous States not being less than three in number including the State in which the permit is issued". [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.87 allows a **temporary permit** (RTA/STA) for up to four months for special occasions, seasonal business, a particular temporary need, or pending renewal. [S1]

### 1.3 Tourist vehicles and the All India Tourist Permit (AITP)
- **FINDING** — The **All India Tourist Vehicles (Permit) Rules, 2023** were notified by G.S.R. 302(E) **dated 18 April 2023**, came into force **1 May 2023**, and superseded the All India Tourist Vehicles (Authorisation or Permit) Rules, 2021. They are made under s.88(14) read with s.88(9) of the MV Act. [S3]
- **FINDING** — Under those Rules, an **AITP is granted only to a tourist vehicle of a tourist vehicle operator** (r.3(1)); "tourist vehicle operator" means "a person who **owns** a tourist vehicle in respect of which an All India Tourist Permit is issued" (r.2(1)(e)). [S3]
- **FINDING** — r.4(4): the AITP is granted in electronic form and "shall be valid for a period of ninety days or its multiples thereof **not exceeding five years** at a time". [S3]
- **FINDING** — r.4(5): an AITP "shall not be granted … after the vehicle has completed **twelve years** from the date of first registration" (ten years for diesel vehicles registered in the NCT of Delhi). [S3]
- **FINDING** — **Permit fee depends on passenger capacity (excluding driver)**. The 2023 Rules' table sets the band **"Ten or more but less than twenty three"** at **₹80,000 per annum** or **₹24,000 per quarter**. A 17-seat vehicle falls in this band. [S3]
- **FINDING** — r.6(1): the AITP "shall be valid throughout the territory of India". r.6(2): it is to be used "for the transport of tourists individually or in a group, along with their personal luggage". [S3]
- **FINDING** — r.10: the operator must "at all times carry a list of tourists … containing the details of origin and the destination of each tourist and … maintain record electronically, of the tourists, including journey details, for a **minimum period of one year**"; r.10(3): "No record of the tourists … shall be shared with any other person or organisation or company." [S3]
- **FINDING** — r.13(1): "The conditions prescribed in **rules 82 to 85A of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 shall not apply** to the All India Tourist Permits granted under these rules." [S3]
- **FINDING** — **DRAFT only:** G.S.R. 630(E) **dated 12 September 2025** proposes to amend the 2023 Rules (among other things: age limit "twelve" → "fifteen"; add general AITP conditions excluding stage-carriage operation and requiring a VLTD/emergency button; require uploading route particulars on VAHAN before each trip). **A draft is not law.** [S4]
- **FINDING** — Where an AITP is not used, a **state tourist permit** may apply under CMVR r.82 (application in **Form 45** to the STA). Under CMVR r.82(2)(a), such a tourist permit is "deemed to be invalid" once a non-motorcab vehicle completes **8 years** unless replaced. [S2]
- **FINDING** — CMVR r.85 (additional conditions for a tourist permit other than a motor cab) requires, among other things: a list of tourist passengers in triplicate with name/address/age/start/destination; the vehicle not to be operated as a stage carriage (r.85(9)); a day-to-day logbook; and 3-monthly returns to the STA. [S2]

### 1.4 Driver licensing
- **FINDING** — MV Act s.3(1): a person shall not drive a motor vehicle in a public place unless holding an effective driving licence; and "no person shall so drive a **transport vehicle** … unless his driving licence **specifically entitles him so to do**". [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.10(2) lists the classes a licence may be expressed for, including (e) **transport vehicle**. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.14(2)(a): a licence to drive a transport vehicle is effective for **five years** (three years where the transport vehicle carries dangerous/hazardous goods). [S1]
- **FINDING** — In **Telangana enforcement**, the State Police e-Challan schedule of "MV Act Offences & Maximum Penalty" lists an offence **"W/o Badge — S 181"** (no driver's badge). This shows a badge is required/enforced for transport vehicles in Telangana. [S8]
- **OPEN QUESTION** — the exact **Telangana Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989** provision that requires a PSV/driver's badge, and whether it applies to a contract-carriage tourist vehicle driven by an owner's employed driver. The Telangana MV Rules index (Chapter II, "Licensing of Drivers") is at [S7]; the Chapter II frames did not render for extraction. Confirm the rule number and application with the Transport Department.
- **FINDING** — Multiple **other States'** motor vehicle rules impose a driver's badge for public service vehicles (e.g. Delhi Transport Department's PSV-badge page; Gujarat MV Rules r.19). These are **other States'** rules, cited only to show the pattern. **COULD NOT VERIFY** a Telangana-specific equivalent text directly. [secondary]

### 1.5 Fitness certification
- **FINDING** — MV Act s.56(1): a transport vehicle "shall not be deemed to be validly registered … unless it carries a **certificate of fitness**" issued by the prescribed authority or an authorised testing station; s.56(5): a fitness certificate "shall, while it remains effective, be valid throughout India"; s.56(4): cancellation of fitness **suspends the registration and any permit** until a new certificate is obtained. [S1]
- **FINDING** — (official MoRTH publication) "For commercial (transport) vehicles, fitness test is required every 2 years for first 8 years, and annually thereafter", and no fitness certificate is granted unless the vehicle is tested at an automated testing station after the notified date. [S17] *(exact renewal cycle to be confirmed with the RTO).*

### 1.6 Insurance
- **FINDING** — MV Act s.146(1): no person shall use (except as a passenger), or cause or allow another to use, a motor vehicle in a public place unless there is in force a policy of insurance complying with Chapter XI. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.147(1)(b)(ii): the policy must insure against "the death of or bodily injury to **any passenger of a transport vehicle**, except gratuitous passengers of a goods vehicle, caused by or arising out of the use of the motor vehicle in a public place". [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.147(2): for third-party insurance related to death or grievous hurt, the Central Government "shall prescribe a base premium and the liability of an insurer in relation to such premium … in consultation with [IRDAI]". [S1]
- **OPEN QUESTION** — what a commercial passenger-carrying policy for a 17-seat contract carriage actually covers and excludes (passenger liability limit, own-damage, driver, unnamed occupants, commercial-use warranty, outstation/interstate use). Only an insurance advisor can answer. (see Q34–Q37)
- **OPEN QUESTION** — whether a policy issued on a **private (non-transport)** registration/use would respond at all if the vehicle is used for hire or reward. This is a classic "policy void for a material change of use" risk and is an insurance-advisor question. (see Q36)

### 1.7 Extra statutory devices on public service vehicles
- **FINDING** — CMVR **r.125H** (inserted by G.S.R. 1095(E) dated 28.11.2016; implementation deferred to 01.04.2019 by S.O. 1663(E) dated 18.04.2018; new-PSV fitment mandated from 01.01.2019 by S.O. 5454(E) dated 25.10.2018) requires all public service vehicles to be equipped with a **vehicle location tracking device (VLTD) and one or more emergency buttons**, to AIS-140 specification. [S2]
- **FINDING** — the **draft** AITP Amendment Rules, 2025 propose to add VLTD/emergency-button fitment as an express condition of an AITP. DRAFT ONLY. [S4]
- **COULD NOT VERIFY (secondary only)** — a reported Supreme Court direction (order said to be dated 13 May 2026) directing States/UTs to enforce r.125H and not to grant fitness under s.56 / permits under s.66 without verified VLTD installation. Reported by a news outlet; **verify against the actual order** before repeating. [S16]

### 1.8 Intermediaries: agent / canvasser / aggregator
- **FINDING** — MV Act s.93(1): no person shall engage himself "(i) as an agent or a canvasser, in the **sale of tickets for travel by public service vehicles or in otherwise soliciting custom for such vehicles** … (iii) as an **aggregator**, unless he has obtained a licence from such authority and subject to such conditions as may be prescribed by the State Government". For an aggregator, the State "may follow such guidelines as may be issued by the Central Government"; every aggregator must also comply with the IT Act, 2000. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.193(1): engaging as an agent/canvasser without a licence is punishable (first offence fine ₹1,000; second/subsequent imprisonment up to six months or fine ₹2,000 or both). s.193(2): engaging as an aggregator without a licence is punishable with fine **up to ₹1,00,000 but not less than ₹25,000**; s.193(3): fine ₹5,000 for breach of a non-material licence condition. [S1]
- **FINDING** — The **Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines, 2020** (MoRTH, issued 27 November 2020; s.36 of the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 brought into force by S.O. 4251(E) dated 26 November 2020) provide a framework for State Governments issuing aggregator licences (eligibility, vehicle/driver compliance, app/website, fare regulation, safety). The guidelines are a **guiding framework for States**; a State licence is the operative requirement. [S10]
- **OPEN QUESTION** — whether the **State of Telangana has notified** its own aggregator licensing rules/portal, and if so their terms. **COULD NOT VERIFY** a Telangana aggregator rules notification from a primary source (only a secondary trade page mentions a Telangana licence fee; not reliable). Confirm with the Transport Department. (see Q30)

### 1.9 Penalties relevant to operating without the right footing
- **FINDING** — s.192A: driving/causing/allowing use of a motor vehicle in contravention of s.66(1), or of a permit condition as to route/area/purpose, is punishable for a first offence with imprisonment up to six months or a fine of ₹10,000, or both. [S1]
- **FINDING** — s.194A: driving a transport vehicle carrying more passengers than authorised in the registration certificate or permit is punishable with a fine of ₹200 per excess passenger. [S1]

### 1.10 GST — headline only (see Q for the CA)
- **FINDING** — GST registration (CGST Act, 2017, s.22(1)) is required once aggregate turnover in a financial year **exceeds ₹20 lakh** for services (₹10 lakh for special-category States; Telangana is not in the ₹10-lakh services list). [S13][S14]
- **FINDING** — Notification No. 10/2017-Integrated Tax dated 13 October 2017 exempts persons making **inter-State supplies of taxable services** whose all-India aggregate turnover does not exceed ₹20 lakh from registration (per GST Council flyer). [S14]
- **FINDING** — CBIC **Circular No. 206/18/2023-GST dated 31 October 2023**: "Services of transport of passengers by any motor vehicle (SAC 9964) and renting of motor vehicle designed to carry passengers **with operator** (SAC 9966), where the cost of fuel is included in the consideration charged from the service recipient attract GST at the rate of **5%** with input tax credit of services in the same line of business." [S11]
- **FINDING** — Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate) (heading 9964) taxes "Transport of passengers … by … air conditioned contract carriage other than motorcab" at 5%, and defines "contract carriage" by MV Act s.2(7). Notification No. 12/2017-CT(R) Sr. No. 15(b) exempts certain **non-air-conditioned** contract carriage passenger transport, **but that exemption expressly excludes tourism, conducted tour, charter or hire**. [S12]
- **OPEN QUESTION** — which head/rate (9964 transport of passengers vs 9966 renting of a motor vehicle with operator) applies to a whole-vehicle charter, whether the vehicle is air-conditioned, and whether the "charter/hire" carve-out removes the exemption. This is a **chartered accountant** question. (see Q31–Q33)

### 1.11 A Telangana statute checked and excluded
- **FINDING** — The **Telangana Public Conveyances Act, 1956** (Act 39 of 1956) requires a licence from the Commissioner of Police to "keep or let for hire any public conveyance", but its definition of "public conveyance" **expressly excludes "a motor vehicle as defined in the Motor Vehicles Act"**. On the text, it does **not** cover a motor-vehicle tourist/contract carriage. (Still worth a lawyer's one-line confirmation.) [S9]

## 2. The four candidate models compared

> For each model: **what it is → typical licences/permits/insurance implicated → what it may and may not lawfully do → key risks.** All as research, to be confirmed.

### Model 1 — Own-vehicle operator (owner operates his own 17-seat vehicle with a driver)
**What it is.** The owner is the registered owner and permit holder; the vehicle is a transport vehicle (contract carriage / tourist vehicle) used for whole-vehicle private charter.

**Typical licences/permits/insurance implicated (FINDING unless stated):**
- Vehicle registered as a **transport vehicle** with a valid **certificate of fitness** (s.56) [S1].
- A valid **permit**: either a **contract carriage permit** (s.73/74, RTA, area-specific, 5-year validity under s.81) **or** a **tourist permit/AITP** (s.88(9) + 2023 Rules; or a State tourist permit via CMVR r.82/Form 45) [S1][S2][S3].
- AITP is granted **only to the owner** (r.3(1)), valid pan-India, 90 days–5 years, ₹80,000/yr for the 10–<23 seat band [S3].
- Valid **insurance** covering third-party and **passengers** (s.146/s.147) [S1].
- Driver holding a **transport-class driving licence** (s.3, s.10(2)(e)); Telangana enforces a **badge** ("W/o Badge — S 181") [S1][S8]; exact Telangana rule = OPEN QUESTION.
- **VLTD + emergency button** under CMVR r.125H [S2].
- Telangana **permit conditions**: issue receipts on demand and maintain a trip sheet in Form TSC (Telangana MV Rules, Chapter V — additional conditions for contract carriages) [S7].
- GST (only if registered — see §1.10) [S13][S14].

**May lawfully do (as research):** hire the whole vehicle to a group on a fixed/agreed sum, on a time or point-to-point basis, without picking up passengers not on the contract [s.2(7)]; carry the contract passengers and their personal luggage.

**May NOT lawfully do (as research):** operate as a **stage carriage** (sell individual/seat-wise tickets, pick up/set down along a route) — s.2(40) requires a stage-carriage permit for that, and CMVR r.85(9) forbids a tourist vehicle being operated as a stage carriage [S1][S2]; and the Telangana Transport Department states on its own site: **"It is illegal to carry passengers on individual tickets in Contract Carriage Buses."** [S6]; operate outside the permit's area/State without countersignature (s.88(1)) [S1]; carry more passengers than registered/permitted (s.194A) [S1].

**Key risks:** operating with no/expired/insufficient permit (s.192A, fine up to ₹10,000/imprisonment) [S1]; operating outside the permit geography; fitness or permit lapse (fitness cancellation suspends both registration and permit, s.56(4)) [S1]; insurance not matching commercial use; passenger-list/trip-sheet non-compliance (AITP r.10; CMVR r.85; Telangana MV Rules) [S2][S3][S7].

### Model 2 — Transport business with subcontracted capacity
**What it is.** The business takes the enquiry/quote and, for trips the own vehicle cannot serve, arranges a third-party commercial vehicle (partner/driver) to perform the trip, possibly under the business's brand.

**Typical licences/permits/insurance implicated (FINDING unless stated):**
- Each **partner vehicle** must have its **own** valid permit, fitness, insurance and a compliant driver — a permit attaches to a specific vehicle/owner (s.66, s.73/74, s.84) [S1]. The business cannot "lend" its own permit to a partner's vehicle.
- If the business solicits custom for public service vehicles for reward, an **agent/canvasser licence under s.93** may be implicated, and if it more than introduces (maps passengers to drivers as a marketplace) potentially an **aggregator licence under s.93(1)(iii)** [S1][S10].
- GST treatment of the subcontracting leg (5% / same-line-of-business ITC per Circular 206) needs a CA [S11].

**May lawfully do (as research):** introduce/broker demand to properly permitted operators; if it operates vehicles itself, only vehicles it holds permits for.

**May NOT lawfully do (as research):** run a third party's unpermitted vehicle on its own permit; represent partner capacity as its own fleet without disclosure/verification; take on a "marketplace matching" role without the s.93(1)(iii) aggregator licence if that is what it is in substance.

**Key risks:** s.192A if any vehicle on the trip lacks the right permit; s.193 exposure if it is an unlicensed agent/aggregator; liability and safety gaps if partner documents are unverified; tax exposure on the subcontracted leg.

### Model 3 — Broker / agent
**What it is.** The business markets and introduces customers to permitted transport operators for a commission/fee; it does not itself operate a vehicle.

**Typical licences/permits/insurance implicated (FINDING unless stated):**
- **s.93(1)(i)** — a licence from the prescribed authority is required to act "as an agent or a canvasser, in the sale of tickets for travel by public service vehicles or in otherwise soliciting custom for such vehicles" [S1].
- Penalty for acting without it: s.193(1) [S1].
- The underlying operator still needs its own permit/fitness/insurance.

**May lawfully do (as research):** with the required licence and lawful contracts, refer enquiries to permitted operators.

**May NOT lawfully do (as research):** solicit custom for public service vehicles or sell travel without the s.93 licence; promise availability the operator has not confirmed.

**Key risks:** s.193 penalty; consumer/disclosure exposure; reputational risk if the operator is non-compliant; GST on commission/brokerage (CA).

### Model 4 — Digital aggregator / marketplace
**What it is.** An online platform where passengers connect with drivers/vehicles, commonly with booking, matching or live inventory.

**Typical licences/permits/insurance implicated (FINDING unless stated):**
- **s.2(1A) "aggregator"** (digital intermediary/marketplace for a passenger to connect with a driver) + **s.93(1)(iii)** — aggregator licence from the State, subject to conditions prescribed by the State, with the State permitted to follow the Central **Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines, 2020** [S1][S10].
- Penalties s.193(2)/(3) [S1].
- Aggregator Guidelines 2020 impose framework obligations (driver/vehicle compliance, app/website, fare regulation, safety) [S10].

**May lawfully do (as research):** operate a licensed marketplace within State-prescribed terms.

**May NOT lawfully do (as research):** operate as an aggregator without the State licence; onboard non-compliant vehicles/drivers.

**Key risks:** s.193 fines ₹25,000–₹1,00,000; State-specific licence conditions; IT Act and consumer-law overlay.
- **OPEN QUESTION** — Whether a **quote-first enquiry site with no live matching, no in-app driver connection and no seat booking** falls within s.2(1A) at all, or whether it is merely a marketing site for a single operator. This is outcome-determinative and must be answered by the advocate and the Transport Department. (Q30)

## 4. (a) What the website MAY safely say today

These are safe because they describe the process or repeat a confirmed fact, and commit to nothing unverified.

1. That the business offers **private group transport / whole-vehicle hire with a driver in Hyderabad**, using a **17-seat Force Urbania** [owner-supplied fact].
2. That it is **not a bus service and not a ticketed route** — the vehicle is hired as a whole for a group ("travels together, no shared passengers"). This aligns with the contract-carriage idea (`s.2(7)`) and with the Telangana Transport Department's own warning against individual-ticket contract carriages [S1][S6]. — **safe as a description of the offering; the legal characterisation is for a lawyer to confirm.**
3. That **availability is confirmed personally for each enquiry** and that **submitting an enquiry does not confirm a booking** — describes the model, guarantees nothing.
4. That a **quotation is prepared from the traveller's itinerary**.
5. That **outstation/interstate feasibility depends on the permits and operating arrangements that apply at the time** — states nothing unverified and is factually true of any operator [S1 s.88].
6. That **luggage/suitability depends on passenger numbers** and will be confirmed.
7. A plain **privacy notice** describing the enquiry data collected and how it is used (see DATA_PRIVACY_CHECKLIST.md).

## 5. (b) What the website MUST NOT say today

1. **No live availability, instant booking, or "seats available"** — the site is quote-first and shows no live inventory by design.
2. **No per-seat fares or individual ticket sales** — this pushes it toward the stage-carriage definition (s.2(40)) and contradicts the Telangana warning [S1][S6].
3. **No claims about permit, insurance, fitness, or compliance status** — all unverified (see `DO_NOT_PUBLISH_UNTIL_VERIFIED.md`).
4. **No claim to be a licensed operator, agent, or aggregator** — none of those licences is confirmed [S1 s.93][S10].
5. **No "fleet", "partner network", "multiple vehicles", or live partner inventory** — only one vehicle exists and partner capacity is unverified.
6. **No statement that outstation, interstate, or airport trips are definitely available/legal** — depends on permits (s.88) [S1].
7. **No GST/tax-invoice claims** ("GST invoice available", "GST registered") — unconfirmed [S13][S14].
8. **No fabricated reviews, ratings, customer counts, awards, or driver-vetting claims**.
