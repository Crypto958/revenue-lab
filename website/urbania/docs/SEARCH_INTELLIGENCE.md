## 1. Market shape (observed, not measured)

- Hyderabad's group-vehicle search space is dominated by **tempo traveller** and **Force Urbania** rental pages. The "17 seater" number is the dominant commercial hook; competitors publish per-km and per-day tables for 12/17/20/26-seat vehicles.
- **Force Urbania is the premium, newer sub-category** and many national players now run dedicated Urbania landing pages. Provider names seen repeatedly across SERPs: Rajputana Cabs, Chiku Cab, ECO Mobility, TaxiYatri, SARA CAB, Urban Cruise, Triveni Cabs, Hyderabad Deccan Tourism, Sathwik Tours & Travels, TransRentals, SimplyTrip, BookMyTempoTraveller, MakeMyTrip.
- **Price framing is the market's core content unit** (₹/km, per-day, driver bata, tolls/parking/state tax as extras, minimum km/day). Rate figures published by competitors are inconsistent and often self-contradictory (see §3) — so published rates are useful only as *relative positioning*, not as verified market rates.
- **The premium angle is under-served in Hyderabad specifically.** Several strong, well-structured Urbania content sites exist (e.g. `urbania.rentals`) but **do not cover Hyderabad at all** (their hubs are Chennai, Bangalore, Mysore, Madurai, Coimbatore). A genuinely Hyderabad-dedicated, single-vehicle Urbania operator can own that gap.
- **Trust signals are the market's weak seam.** Almost every competitor claims star ratings, review counts and "no hidden charges" with no verifiable source, while few show the driver's commercial licence, the vehicle's permit/insurance, first-aid kit, or real vehicle photos. A single-vehicle operator can win on verifiable specificity (this vehicle, this driver, these documents).

## 3. Competitor observations (only domains actually fetched)

Detailed notes per domain are in `COMPETITOR_NOTES.md`. Headline observations:

- **Page types are fairly uniform:** city + vehicle landing pages, per-km/per-day fare tables, "how to book" step blocks, FAQ blocks, and (for premium players) Urbania-vs-Tempo-Traveller comparison tables.
- **Conversion patterns are phone/WhatsApp-first**, with online quote forms as a secondary path. App-download funnels appear on the cab-aggregator brands (Chiku Cab, MakeMyTrip).
- **Content quality is uneven.** Several Hyderabad pages carry typos, self-contradictory prices, and generic template copy reused across dozens of cities. This is the exploitable gap for a credible single-vehicle operator.
- **No competitor I fetched is honest about fleet size or availability.** All imply multi-vehicle fleets; none describe what happens when the single vehicle is already booked. That is a genuine, ownable content gap.

Domains fetched and reviewed (see `COMPETITOR_NOTES.md` for per-domain detail):
`hyderabaddeccantourism.com`, `rajputanacabs.in`, `chikucab.com`, `sara.cab`, `sttforceurbaniarentalhyderabad.com`, `ecosmobility.com`, `mebus.in`, `transrentals.in`, `urbania.rentals`, `taxiyatri.com`, `trivenicabs.in`, `urbancruise.in`, `bookmytempotraveller.com`, `simplytrip.in`, `makemytrip.com`, and `justdial.com` (attempted — see COULD NOT VERIFY).

## 5. Official-source research answers

Every item below is marked **FINDING** (verified in the official page I fetched, with exact URL) or **COULD NOT VERIFY**.

### 5.1 Google — official sources

**(a) Service-area businesses with no customer-facing premises — FINDING.**
Source: Google Business Profile Help, "Manage your service areas for service-area & hybrid businesses" — `https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481`
What the page states (paraphrase of the fetched text, not invented quotes):
- A **service-area business** is "a business that visits or delivers to customers directly but doesn't serve customers at their business address" (examples given: cleaning services, plumbers). A transport/hire business that visits customers fits this pattern; **if you don't serve customers at your business address, remove your address from your Business Profile.**
- **Service-area businesses can only have one profile for the whole area they serve.**
- You **cannot** set a radius; you must specify the area by **city, postal code, or another type of area**, up to **20 service areas**, and the overall area "shouldn't be more than about 2 hours of driving time from where your business is based."
- A **hybrid business** serves customers at its address *and* visits them; if it has no permanent on-site signage it is not eligible as a storefront and should be listed as a service-area business.

**(b) FAQ structured data eligibility — FINDING (effectively: no site is eligible).**
Sources: Google Search Central documentation updates — `https://developers.google.com/search/updates`
- The May 2026 changelog entry **"Deprecating the FAQ rich result feature"** states the feature "will no longer appear in Google Search starting May 7, 2026."
- The June 15, 2026 changelog entry **"Removing documentation for the FAQ rich result feature"** states: "The FAQ rich result feature is no longer shown in Google Search results, as announced in the changelog entry in May 2026."
- I attempted to fetch the FAQ doc directly (`https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage`) and **the URL served the documentation-updates page instead**, consistent with the doc having been removed. **Practical consequence:** FAQPage markup no longer earns a rich result for any site; it remains a valid schema.org type and Google continues to parse it, but it should not be treated as a Google SERP feature. (The Q&A *content* can still be indexed and cited by AI features — see CONTENT_GAPS.)

**(c) Local business structured data requirements — FINDING (with one interpretive caution).**
Source: Google Search Central, "Local business (LocalBusiness) structured data" — `https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business` (page states "Last updated 2026-09-08 UTC").
- **Required properties:** `address` (`PostalAddress`, described as "the physical location of the business") and `name`. Google states: "You must include the required properties for your content to be eligible for display as a rich result." You must use "the most specific `LocalBusiness` sub-type possible."
- **Recommended properties:** telephone, url, geo/geo.latitude, geo.longitude, openingHoursSpecification, priceRange, department, and more. `aggregateRating`/`review` are "only recommended for sites that capture reviews about other local businesses."
- "You can add `LocalBusiness` structured data to any page on your site."
- **CAUTION (my judgement, not a Google statement):** the LocalBusiness doc lists `address` as a **required** property and describes it as the business's physical location; the page does **not** carve out an exception for service-area businesses. So a one-vehicle business with no customer-facing premises may not satisfy the documented required property if it publishes no address. This is the operator's decision to take on advice; it is **not** a Google-published rule about service-area LocalBusiness markup that I could verify.

**Also relevant — FINDING.** Google Search Central, "Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search" — `https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide` (page states "Last updated 2026-07-10 UTC"):
- SEO best practices remain relevant for generative AI features (they are grounded in core Search ranking/quality systems, using retrieval-augmented generation and "query fan-out").
- Create **non-commodity** content with a unique point of view; avoid scaled content abuse.
- Explicitly listed as things to ignore for Google Search: "chunking" content, creating "unnecessary AI text files (like llms.txt)," and pursuing inauthentic mentions.
- To be eligible for generative AI features the site must be indexed/eligible with a snippet, and (per the page) "a site must be included in Search generative AI features in Search Console."
- Local/ecommerce visibility is directed through **Google Business Profiles** and Merchant Center.

### 5.2 OpenAI — official sources

**(a) OAI-SearchBot — FINDING.**
Source: OpenAI, "Overview of OpenAI Crawlers" — `https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots`
- OAI-SearchBot "is for search… used to surface websites in search results in ChatGPT's search features."
- "Sites that are opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though can still appear as navigational links."
- OpenAI recommends "allowing OAI-SearchBot in your site's robots.txt file and allowing requests from our published IP ranges" (IP list: `https://openai.com/searchbot.json`).
- OAI-SearchBot and GPTBot are **independent** settings; a site can allow OAI-SearchBot (appear in search) while disallowing GPTBot (opt out of training).
- "For search results… it can take ~24 hours from a site's robots.txt update for our systems to adjust."

**(b) Allowing AI answer engines to cite a site — FINDING.**
Sources:
- OpenAI Help Center, "Searching the web with ChatGPT" — `https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt`:
  - "To make a website eligible for inclusion, allow OAI-Searchbot to crawl the site and confirm that the website host or content delivery network allows traffic from OpenAI's published searchbot IP addresses."
  - "ChatGPT ranks search results using multiple factors intended to help users find relevant, reliable information. **Placement is not guaranteed.**"
  - ChatGPT may use approximate (IP-based) location for local results, and users can add city/postcode to improve local results — relevant because many queries here are Hyderabad-local.
  - Responses using search show inline citations and a **Sources** panel.
- OpenAI, "Introducing ChatGPT search" — `https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-search/`:
  - "Any website or publisher can choose to appear in ChatGPT search" (links to `https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots`).

## 6. What this implies for the site (short)

1. Build one deep Hyderabad Urbania page plus the use-case pages in `KEYWORD_MAP.csv`, and make the **single-vehicle, pre-booked** model explicit.
2. Lead with verifiable specificity (this vehicle, this driver, these documents, actual photos) because competitors rely on unverifiable trust claims.
3. Answer the question-form queries in §4 with clean, extractable, factual Q&A content — valuable for AI answer engines even though FAQ *rich results* are gone.
4. Do not fabricate rates or volumes; publish an honest "how your quote is built" explainer instead of a false rate card.
5. Fix robots.txt so OAI-SearchBot (and other AI crawlers) are allowed, per §5.2.
