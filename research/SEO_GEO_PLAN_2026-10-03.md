## 4. GEO / AI-SEARCH BEST PRACTICES (2026)

### 4.1 Google's own line (FACT)
Google states its generative features are rooted in core Search ranking/quality systems and rely on retrieval-augmented generation plus "query fan-out", and that **"no special optimization is required"** — foundational SEO covers it [1][2]. Requirements to be *eligible* as a supporting link: the page must be indexed and snippet-eligible; create valuable, **non-commodity** content; good page experience [1][6]. Structured data is still recommended and **must match visible content** [3][6].

### 4.2 llms.txt — low cost, no proven payoff (FACT)
- It is a **proposed, not official**, standard (created Sept 2024); it's a content map, not a crawl-control file [5][14].
- **No major AI provider has formally adopted it.** Google's Gary Illyes: "We currently have no plans to support LLMs.txt"; John Mueller: no AI system currently uses it [5][14][15].
- Anthropic and Perplexity *say* their retrieval reads it; OpenAI's GPTBot fetches it without committing to use it [5].
- Adoption: ~**10.13% of ~300k domains** (SE Ranking), vs 92.8% using robots.txt; **no statistically significant correlation with AI citation rates** [5][19][14].
- Verdict (ESTIMATE): implement it cheaply (harmless, forward-compatible, some platforms read it) — but **do not** treat it as a citation lever. It does not replace robots.txt [5][9].

### 4.3 robots.txt treatment of AI bots (FACT)
Two/three bot families per provider — the choice matters:

| Bot | Owner | Type | robots.txt behavior | Westbridge rule |
|-----|-------|------|---------------------|-----------------|
| GPTBot | OpenAI | Training | Honors | **Allow** (brand familiarity) |
| OAI-SearchBot | OpenAI | Search/index | Honors (shares crawl with GPTBot since Dec 2025) | **Allow** (ChatGPT citations) |
| ChatGPT-User | OpenAI | User-triggered fetch | **No longer reliably honors** robots.txt | Allow (can't reliably block anyway) [9][10] |
| ClaudeBot | Anthropic | Training | Honors | Allow |
| Claude-SearchBot | Anthropic | Search/index | Honors | **Allow** (Claude citations) |
| Claude-User | Anthropic | User-triggered | Honors per docs | Allow |
| PerplexityBot | Perplexity | Search/index | Honors ("stealth crawler" caveat) | **Allow** [9][10] |
| Google-Extended | Google | Training token (no HTTP fetch) | Opt-out token only | **Allow** — blocking does **not** affect Search ranking [10] |
| CCBot | Common Crawl | Training corpus | Honors | Allow (feeds many downstream models) |
| Bytespider | ByteDance | Training | Ignores; needs WAF | Disallow (optional) |

For a lead-gen site, **allow everything** — you *want* citations. Only user-triggered fetchers can't be reliably blocked (server/WAF-level only) [9].

### 4.4 Structured data that AI systems actually consume (FACT + caveat)
- ~**65% of pages cited by Google AI Mode and ~71% cited by ChatGPT** carry schema; the dominant types on AI-cited pages are **BreadcrumbList (38–42%), WebSite (34–36%), Organization (31–32%), WebPage (31–36%)** — i.e. *site/entity* schemas, not content schemas [7].
- AccuraCast (2,000 prompts / 9,000 citations across ChatGPT, AI Overviews, Perplexity): **81% of cited pages had some schema**, but they conclude schema "matters to a very limited extent as a ranking, visibility, or citation factor." **Person schema = 58.9%** of cited pages (70.4% on ChatGPT); **FAQPage = just 1.8%** [12].
- Interpretation (ESTIMATE): schema is table-stakes/correlation, not a citation cause; the winning pattern is **rich entity graphs** — `Organization`/`LocalBusiness` + `sameAs`, `Person` (named, credentialed author), `Service`, `BreadcrumbList`, `WebSite`, `WebPage`, `Article`. FAQPage markup is still worth adding for *user-facing* Q&A blocks and possible rich results, but shouldn't be relied on for GEO [7][12].
- Google: supported types only, and markup **must mirror visible content** [3][6]. Format: **JSON-LD** (Google's recommended format) [Google structured-data intro].

### 4.5 Content patterns that move AI citations (FACT)
The peer-reviewed **GEO paper** (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) tested 9 edits over ~10,000 queries: **Cite Sources, Quotation Addition, and Statistics Addition** lifted share-of-answer by **up to ~40%** (Position-Adjusted Word Count), while keyword stuffing did *worse* than baseline [8][13]. Practical rules:
- **Answer-first**: open every page and each H2 with a **40–60 word self-contained answer capsule** (definition = "[Entity] is a [category] that [differentiator]") — each block should stand alone as a retrieval unit [20][21].
- **Statistics density**: one concrete, verifiable number every ~150–200 words; replace vague claims with figures [8][21].
- **Quotations/citations**: name the source, institution, year; link to primary sources (Google docs, schema.org, studies) [8][13].
- **Entity clarity**: one canonical entity per page; consistent brand name; `sameAs` to profiles; mention adjacent entities (ChatGPT, Perplexity, WhatsApp API) to build semantic context [21][11].
- **Structure**: question-style H2/H3, 200–400-word sections, numbered lists, comparison tables — extractable units [20][21].
- **Freshness**: update core pages monthly (even a data point/timestamp); AI engines favor fresher content [20][21].
- **E-E-A-T**: trust is the foundational quality signal; show author identity/credentials, About, contact, sourcing [11].

### 4.6 Off-site / corroboration (FACT + ESTIMATE)
AI engines run three tests — **identity, extractability, corroboration** [20]. Beyond the site: consistent NAP + fresh **Google Business Profile** (GBP signals ~32–36% of local-pack weight) [17]; presence in third-party listicles, news/digital PR, relevant subreddits and a Wikipedia-grade entity are the fastest citation levers [20]. **ESTIMATE:** for a Hyderabad SMB service, GBP + industry-directory citations + a handful of local press mentions are the highest-ROI off-site moves.

## 5. RECOMMENDED SITE STRUCTURE & ANSWER-FIRST TEMPLATES

### 5.1 Structure (hub-and-spoke, static HTML + JSON-LD)
```
/                         Home  — H1: "Never lose another enquiry: AI lead-response for Hyderabad businesses"
/hyderabad/               Local hub — H1: "AI lead-response & AI enablement in Hyderabad"
/services/
   ai-receptionist-india/     H1: "AI receptionist in India: answer every call, 24/7"
   whatsapp-automation-india/ H1: "WhatsApp automation for Indian businesses"
   lead-follow-up-automation/ H1: "Automate lead follow-up so no enquiry goes cold"
   enquiry-management/        H1: "Customer enquiry management, done for you"
   done-for-you-enquiry-response/ H1: "Done-for-you enquiry response"
/ai-for-local-business/    Pillar — H1: "AI for local business: a practical 2026 guide"
/ai-enablement/            Pillar — H1: "AI enablement for small businesses in India"
/for-clinics/  /for-salons-spas/  /for-gyms/  /for-wedding-venues/  /for-hotels-banquets/  /for-coaching-centres/  /for-home-services/  (vertical spokes)
/guides/
   true-cost-of-a-missed-call/  H1: "How much does a missed call really cost?"
   lead-response-time/          H1: "Lead response time: how fast is fast enough?"
   revive-dead-leads/           H1: "How to revive leads that went cold"
/pricing/  /about/ (E-E-A-T: team, credentials, Hyderabad)  /contact/
/case-studies/[...]  /faq/ (master FAQ hub)
```
**Internal linking:** Home → both pillars + `/hyderabad/`; each pillar → its service pages + 2–3 vertical spokes; each service page → relevant guide, `/pricing/`, `/contact/`, and back to the pillar; every vertical spoke → the matching service + city hub. Use descriptive anchors ("WhatsApp automation for clinics"), not "click here". Every page links up to its hub; hubs link down to all spokes (topical authority) [21].

### 5.2 Prioritised answer-first paragraph templates
Each = a 40–60-word capsule under a question-style H2, so it is extractable verbatim by AI engines and earns a snippet. Priority order:

1. **Definition / "what is"** (service + pillar pages) — *"An AI receptionist is a voice agent that answers your business line 24/7, understands caller intent, books appointments, and sends WhatsApp confirmations — so no enquiry is missed."*
2. **Cost / "how much"** (`/pricing/`, comparison) — lead with a number and range: *"AI lead-response for an Indian small business typically runs ₹1,500–₹5,000/month in platform costs, or a done-for-you setup from ₹10,000–₹50,000 plus a ₹4,000–₹40,000/month retainer."* (Westbridge's own pricing = FACT; market ranges ESTIMATE [18][22].)
3. **Problem/impact / "how much does X cost me"** (guide pages) — open with the statistic: *"Small businesses leave ~62% of inbound calls unanswered, and ~85% of missed callers never call back — an average loss near $126,000/year."* [4]
4. **"How to" / process** (service pages) — numbered steps, 3–5.
5. **Comparison** ("vs") — table: AI receptionist vs chatbot vs agency [12].
6. **Local** ("in Hyderabad") — city-specific capsule + GBP, area names (HITEC City, Banjara Hills, Gachibowli, Kukatpally).
7. **Vertical FAQ** — one capsule per vertical ("Can AI book appointments for my dental clinic?").
8. **Trust / E-E-A-T** — named author + credentials + About capsule.

**Quotable-stat assets to seed (FACT):** 62% unanswered [4]; 85% never call back [4]; ~$126k/yr loss [4]; HBR — 7× more likely to qualify a lead if contacted within an hour [23][24]; GEO — +30–40% from statistics/quotations/citations [8][13]; India ~535M WhatsApp users [16]; GBP ~32–36% of local-pack weight [17].
